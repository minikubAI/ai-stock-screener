This isn't because these companies are failing. Japan's corporate governance revolution is forcing companies to return cash to shareholders at record rates. Share buybacks hit ¥17 trillion in 2025. Dividends are climbing year after year. The market just hasn't caught up yet — especially for smaller companies that fly under the radar of international investors.

Warren Buffett saw it. He put billions into Japanese trading houses. Since then, those investments have roughly doubled. But the real opportunity isn't in the large caps that Buffett bought — it's in the thousands of small and mid-cap companies that no English-language analyst covers.

The information gap is the edge. Most Japanese company filings exist only in Japanese. Earnings reports, management commentary, shareholder letters — all in Japanese. Our system reads them all, translates the signals into data, and surfaces the companies worth your attention.

What You Need to Know

Essential context for investing in Japanese equities from abroad

The TSE Revolution

Tokyo Stock Exchange now requires companies below book value to publish capital improvement plans. Non-compliant companies risk delisting. Result: record buybacks and dividend hikes across the board.

The Currency Angle

The weak yen makes Japanese stocks cheaper for dollar/euro buyers. If the yen recovers, you capture both stock gains AND currency gains — a potential double tailwind.

Start With $7

Through international brokers, you can buy single shares of Japanese stocks (no 100-share minimum). Build a diversified portfolio of 10+ stocks with under $100.

The Small Cap Edge

Even Japanese analysts barely cover companies under ¥50B market cap. Zero English coverage means systematic screening has maximum edge here — exactly where our system focuses.
