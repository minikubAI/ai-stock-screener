1. Why Invest in Japanese Stocks Now

Japan's equity market is experiencing a structural transformation that hasn't been seen in decades. The Tokyo Stock Exchange has taken the unprecedented step of publicly pressuring listed companies to improve their capital efficiency — specifically targeting the nearly 50% of companies that trade below book value.

The results are already visible: record share buybacks, dividend increases, and corporate restructuring. Yet many of these improving companies, particularly in the small and mid-cap space, receive virtually no coverage in English. This information asymmetry is the opportunity.

Key fact: In 2025, Japanese companies announced a record ¥17 trillion in share buybacks. Many small caps yielding 3–5% dividends trade at single-digit PERs — valuations that would be impossible in the U.S. market.

3. Opening Your Account — Step by Step

We'll use Interactive Brokers as the example, since it's available in the most countries and has the best Japanese stock access.

1

Go to the IBKR website and click "Open Account"

Choose "Individual" account type. You'll need your passport or government-issued ID, proof of address (utility bill or bank statement), and your tax identification number.

2

Complete the application form

Fill in personal details, financial information, and investment experience. Under "Trading Permissions," make sure to enable "Japan — Stocks". This grants access to TSE-listed securities.

3

Submit identity verification

Upload your passport photo and proof of address. IBKR typically verifies within 1–2 business days. Some countries may require additional documentation.

4

Fund your account

Transfer funds via bank wire, ACH (US), or other supported methods. You can fund in USD and convert to JPY within IBKR at interbank rates (much better than your bank's exchange rate).

5

Convert currency to JPY

In IBKR Trader Workstation or the web portal, go to the currency conversion tool. Convert your USD/EUR to JPY. The spread is typically less than 0.002% — far cheaper than any bank.

6

Place your first order

Search for any Japanese stock by its 4-digit code (e.g., "7203" for Toyota) or by name. Select the TSE venue, enter quantity, and submit. TSE trading hours are 9:00–15:00 JST (with a lunch break 11:30–12:30).

Tip: IBKR offers a paper trading account where you can practice buying Japanese stocks with virtual money before committing real capital.

5. Fractional Shares (Mini-Kabu)

In Japan, stocks traditionally trade in units of 100 shares. A stock priced at ¥3,000 would require ¥300,000 (~$2,000) for a minimum purchase — a significant barrier.

Fractional shares (ミニ株 / mini-kabu) solve this by allowing you to buy as little as 1 share. This means you can start investing in Japanese stocks with as little as ¥1,000 (~$7).

Through IBKR, you can buy individual shares of any TSE-listed stock — not just the standard 100-share units. This effectively gives foreign investors access to the same flexibility that Japanese retail investors have through domestic mini-kabu services.

Note: When buying fewer than 100 shares ("odd lots"), you may have reduced voting rights and slightly wider bid-ask spreads. For investment purposes, this is usually not a concern.

6. Tax Considerations

Tax treatment varies significantly by your country of residence. Here are the key points to be aware of:

Dividend withholding tax: Japan withholds 15.315% tax on dividends paid to non-residents. Depending on the tax treaty between Japan and your country, this may be reduced (often to 10%) and can usually be claimed as a foreign tax credit on your domestic tax return.

Capital gains: Japan generally does not tax capital gains earned by non-residents on Japanese stocks. Your gains will be taxed according to your home country's rules.

Currency gains/losses: Fluctuations in the JPY/USD (or JPY/EUR) rate will affect your total return. A stock that gains 10% in JPY terms but sees the yen weaken 5% against the dollar yields roughly 5% in dollar terms. This works both ways.

Recommendation: Consult a tax professional in your country of residence for specific advice. Tax treaties with Japan vary widely.

7. Practical Tips

Start small and diversify

With fractional shares, you can spread $500 across 10–15 different stocks. Concentration in a single name is the most common mistake new investors in Japanese stocks make.

Understand the trading hours

TSE trades 9:00–11:30 and 12:30–15:00 Japan Standard Time (JST = UTC+9). That's 0:00–2:30 and 3:30–6:00 in New York (EST), or 5:00–7:30 and 8:30–11:00 in London. You can place limit orders outside these hours.

Watch the yen

Currency is a significant factor. Many foreign investors in Japanese stocks benefit from timing their JPY conversion — buying yen when it's weak, and selling Japanese stocks when the yen is strong, to capture both equity gains and currency gains.
