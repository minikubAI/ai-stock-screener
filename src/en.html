Why Japanese Stocks, Why Now

Japan is the world's most overlooked developed market. Nearly half of all listed companies on the Tokyo Stock Exchange trade below book value — a situation unthinkable in the U.S. or Europe. The Tokyo Stock Exchange has publicly pressured these companies to improve capital efficiency or face potential delisting, triggering a historic wave of buybacks and dividend increases.

Warren Buffett's well-publicized investments in Japan's trading houses were an early signal. Since then, foreign investment in Japanese equities has surged. Yet the information gap remains: most company filings are in Japanese only, and English-language coverage of small and mid-cap Japanese stocks is virtually nonexistent.

That gap is what this project aims to fill — using systematic screening to identify the opportunities that language barriers hide from global investors.

As of 2026, approximately 50% of TSE-listed companies trade below a price-to-book ratio of 1.0 — meaning the market values them at less than their net assets. Many are profitable, cash-rich, and increasingly returning capital to shareholders.

Key Context for Foreign Investors

Things you should know about investing in Japanese equities

TSE Reforms

Since 2023, the Tokyo Stock Exchange has required companies trading below book value to publish improvement plans. This is driving unprecedented shareholder returns.

Currency Factor

A weaker yen makes Japanese stocks cheaper for foreign buyers in USD/EUR terms. Returns should be evaluated in both JPY and your home currency.

How to Buy

Foreign investors can access Japanese stocks through Interactive Brokers, Saxo Bank, or other international brokers offering TSE access.

Small Cap Edge

Analyst coverage of Japanese small caps is minimal even domestically. Companies under ¥50B market cap are frequently mispriced — this is where systematic screening has the biggest advantage.
